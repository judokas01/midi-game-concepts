# King Gong: public-domain song ideas

Simple tunes known worldwide and in the Czech Republic that the game could add, with a
rating for how hard each is to play. Tick the ones you want. "(in game)" marks songs King
Gong already has: the first ten in scripts/song.gd, the rest in its song book,
scripts/songbook/. "(written out)" marks songs whose notes are at the end of this file.

**Public domain** here means a traditional song, or music whose composer died more than 70
years ago (and, for the US, that was published in 1930 or earlier). This is a rule of thumb,
not legal advice.

**What suits the game:** one note at a time, a simple rhythm, and a range of about an octave.
The computer keys play C4 to C5. "(over an octave)" marks a tune that needs a note moved, or
can be played only on a MIDI keyboard. Each song's range gets checked when it is written out.

## How hard

The rating is for playing the tune alone, one note at a time, as the game asks. The chord
modes add to it. The ratings are estimates; each is confirmed when the song is written out.
Within each section, the songs run from easiest to hardest.

| Rating | Meaning | For example |
|---|---|---|
| ★☆☆☆☆ | A few neighbouring notes, an even rhythm | Mary Had a Little Lamb |
| ★★☆☆☆ | Easy: mostly steps within five or six notes, a plain rhythm | Twinkle Twinkle, Ode to Joy |
| ★★★☆☆ | Medium: about an octave, some leaps, dotted or quick notes, the odd black key | Happy Birthday, Korobeiniki |
| ★★★★☆ | Hard: fast runs, wide leaps, many black keys or off-beat rhythms | Für Elise, Can-can |
| ★★★★★ | Very hard: fast and chromatic, or far wider than an octave | Rondo alla Turca, Flight of the Bumblebee |

- **Škoda lásky:** Jaromír Vejvoda wrote it in 1927, and he died in 1988.
- **Katyusha:** written in 1938.
- **Moscow Nights:** written in 1955.
- **Kookaburra:** written in 1932.

  Jingle Bell Rock.
- **Musicals and newer children's songs:** Do-Re-Mi, Edelweiss, and the well-known Baby Shark
  version.
- **Film scores and film songs:** Star Wars, Harry Potter, Titanic's My Heart Will Go On, The
  Godfather, Jaws, Mission: Impossible, The Pink Panther, James Bond, every Disney song, Over
  the Rainbow and The Lion Sleeps Tonight.
- **Classical-sounding but modern:** Albinoni's Adagio (Remo Giazotto wrote it in 1958), O
  Fortuna from Carmina Burana (Orff, 1937; heard in Excalibur), Barber's Adagio for Strings
  (1936; heard in Platoon), Khachaturian's Sabre Dance (1942) and Shostakovich's Waltz No. 2
  (heard in Eyes Wide Shut).
- **Free in Europe but not yet in the US:** Prokofiev's Dance of the Knights and Peter and the
  Wolf, and Gershwin's Summertime.
- **Czech songs by Uhlíř and Svěrák:** Dělání, Holubí dům, Není nutno and Když jde malý bobr
  spát.
- **Czech film and TV songs:** Tři oříšky pro Popelku, Pyšná princezna, and the songs from
  Krtek, Večerníček and the other fairy-tale films.
- **Later Czech lyrics:** words written later for an old tune, like Zelené pláně, can still be
  under copyright. The game uses only the tune, so this doesn't matter here.
